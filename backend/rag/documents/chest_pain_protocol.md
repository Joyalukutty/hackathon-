# Acute Chest Pain Clinical Pathway (Synthetic Demo)

## Overview
Guidelines for assessing and managing patients presenting with acute chest pain in the ED.

## Immediate Assessment (Within 10 minutes)
- Obtain and interpret 12-lead ECG.
- Vital signs including O2 saturation.
- Establish IV access.
- Brief targeted history and physical examination.

## Risk Stratification
- **High Risk:** ST-segment elevation on ECG (STEMI), hemodynamic instability, or refractory ischemic chest pain.
- **Moderate Risk:** Positive cardiac biomarkers (troponin) without ST elevation (NSTEMI), dynamic ECG changes.
- **Low Risk:** Normal initial ECG and biomarkers, absence of high-risk historical features.

## Initial Interventions
- Aspirin 162-325 mg chewed (if no contraindications).
- Nitroglycerin sublingual for active pain (avoid if right ventricular infarction suspected or recent PDE5 inhibitor use).
- Oxygen only if O2 sat < 90%.
- Morphine or fentanyl for pain refractory to nitroglycerin.
